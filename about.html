Introduction to Tout-Fait

Tout-Fait launched in 1999 through the Art Science Research Laboratory, a not-for-profit publisher, as the first academic journal devoted entirely to Marcel Duchamp (1887–1968) and the artists around him — and the first of its kind to appear purely online. Its name is a small piece of wordplay: tout fait is the standard French rendering of "readymade," a term with roots in a phrase used by the mathematician Henri Poincaré, whose thinking shaped Duchamp's own. The name signals what the journal has tried to be ever since — a meeting point between art and science, and a home for scholarship that doesn't sit neatly inside one discipline.

The journal draws on writers and researchers across art history and the sciences, publishing news features, full-length articles, interviews, and shorter notes on Duchamp and his circle. Free of print's space constraints, it has been able to run longer, more exploratory pieces than a print journal typically could, and to host conversation between senior scholars, emerging researchers, and interested general readers alike.

Over the years the journal has become a recognized reference point in Duchamp studies and modern art history more broadly, with a substantial and steadily growing readership. Since transitioning to a rolling publication model in 2005, new peer-reviewed submissions are added as they're accepted, alongside ongoing features like search, reader comments, and news coverage.

Tout-Fait remains strictly not-for-profit, run by a small team of writers, editors, and developers, and its continued operation depends on reader support and contributor submissions. If you'd like to support the journal financially, see the Help Scholar page; if you'd like to submit research or a creative project, see Call for Papers / Submissions for guidelines.
